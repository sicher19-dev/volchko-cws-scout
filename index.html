Volchko's weapon set

6'4" RHP, ~3.8 ERA, 98 K. A hard supinator who creates plus glove-side shapes and almost no arm-side life. As a righty, everything he throws moves to the first-base (glove) side — away from right-handed bats, in toward left-handed bats. That movement profile, not handedness alone, drives every plan below.

Core read — get this right and the plan writes itself: Volchko's glove-side pitches (cutter, slider, curve) all break toward first base — so they run away from right-handed hitters and in on left-handed hitters. He has essentially no arm-side life, which means two hard limits: he can't run anything in on a righty's hands, and he can't run anything away from a lefty. That makes the 5 RHH the more pitchable group (work them away and down), and the 4 lefties — Anthony Pack Jr. above all — the ones who can sit out over the plate where his stuff can't reach. The cutter that backs up over the middle is the pitch that beats him.

Macro plan

The 5 right-handed bats

Robbins · Tinney · Becerra · Mendoza · Borba
  • The pitchable group. Cutter and slider break away off the barrel — live on the outer third and the bottom of the zone.
  • Expand down & away with the curve for chase; the power RHH (Borba 30% K, Tinney/Robbins ~22%) swing through it.
  • He can't run anything in on their hands — so the only in-pitch is an elevated four-seam to change the eye, never a cutter (it backs up over the middle).
  • vs pull-heavy Borba, keep the cutter off the outer edge and never let it leak to his pull side.
  • Exception: Mendoza (11.8% K, 14.9% BB) won't chase — attack the zone away, accept weak contact, don't waste spin beneath it.

The 4 left-handed bats

Pack · Rodriguez (switch) · Larson · Pendergrass
  • The structural hole: nothing runs away from a lefty. They can lean out over the plate; anything middle-away runs back to the barrel.
  • The cutter is still a weapon — it jams them in on the hands. Pitch them in and up, backfoot the slider/curve.
  • Pack Jr. — the toughest AB in the order. Take the outer third out of the plan entirely; jam in, climb with the four-seam, never a cutter over the middle.
  • Larson & Pendergrass have almost no power (1 & 0 HR) — just throw strikes; Larson's 20.5% walk rate punishes any nibble.
