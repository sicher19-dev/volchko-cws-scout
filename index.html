Volchko's weapon set

6'4" RHP — coming off a complete-game, 15-K shutdown of Texas. A hard supinator who creates plus glove-side shapes and almost no arm-side life. As a righty, everything he throws moves to the first-base (glove) side — away from right-handed bats, in toward left-handed bats. That movement profile, not handedness alone, drives every plan below.

Core read — and why Oklahoma is the harder draw than Texas: Volchko's glove-side pitches (cutter, slider, curve) all break toward first base — away from righties, in on lefties — and he has essentially no arm-side life, so he can't run anything away from a left-handed hitter. Texas gave him 5 RHH to break away from. Oklahoma stacks six left-handed bats (Walk & Willits switch; C. Johnson, Gambill, Harris, Tockey from the left) across the top and middle of the order — the worst-case handedness draw for his profile, because they can collectively sit out over the plate where his stuff can't reach. The plan against the lefties: take the outer third off the table, jam in with the cutter, backfoot the slider/curve, elevate the four-seam — and never let the cutter back up over the middle. The three righties (Lachance, Brock, Branch) are the comfortable group — but Lachance is the best hitter on the team.

Macro plan

The 6 left-handed bats — the challenge

Walk · C. Johnson · Willits · Gambill · Harris · Tockey
  • The defining problem: nothing Volchko throws runs away from a lefty, and Oklahoma stacks six of them. They can lean out over the plate; anything middle-away runs back to the barrel.
  • The cutter is the weapon — it jams them in on the hands. Pitch them in and up, backfoot the slider/curve, never leak the cutter over the middle.
  • The patient ones (Gambill .445 OBP, Tockey 21% BB, Willits .407 OBP) won't chase — beat them in the zone, in; don't nibble into walks.
  • The whiff lefties (Tockey 28% K, Walk 25%) chase the backfoot breaker down-and-in.
  • C. Johnson (9 HR / 26 SB) and Willits are the dangerous lefties — respect them, don't groove the in-cutter, and mind the run game.

The 3 right-handed bats — the comfort zone

Lachance · Brock · Branch
  • Where the profile plays up — glove-side stuff runs away off the barrel. Work the outer third and expand down.
  • Lachance is the exception — the team's best hitter (.330 / .600, 14 HR) at just 17% K. Pitch him away with care; never elevate into that power.
  • Brock (27% K, 12 HR / 26 SB) is the chase target — away and down, then respect the legs if he reaches.
  • Branch (.228, .084 ISO) is the easiest out in the order — attack the zone, don't overthink the nine-hole.
